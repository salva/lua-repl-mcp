Operate safely

Choose cwd and output

session_create options or execute's create_options select cwd, stdout and stderr paths. Paths resolve against the selected cwd. Output paths must not already exist; they are exclusively created, never overwritten. Stdout/stderr may share one newly created file. Startup failure may leave reported partial created paths; inspect those before retrying. Defaults send output to /dev/null. Printed output never automatically becomes MCP results. Protocol stdout must contain only newline-delimited JSON-RPC, not diagnostics.

Lifecycle and shutdown

Sessions are owned by the client connection. EOF/disconnect initiates disposal. SIGINT/SIGTERM request bounded manager shutdown. An independent POSIX guardian owns worker spawn/reap and supervises lifetime on abrupt manager death, including blocked Lua/native work. Progressive disposal retries TERM over 20 seconds, then KILL over 10 seconds at one-second intervals, stopping on confirmed exit. At the 30-second total bound, unconfirmed children are abandoned with warnings and resources released. Inspect confirmation; best effort is not proof of exit. Guardian failure is not a promise of automatic worker disposal in every OS failure. Arbitrary descendant process trees are not sandboxed or guaranteed cleanup.

Embedded hosts own their own lifecycle: manager signal/kill policy does not apply to them. Close returns control without closing handles or clearing state.

Client deadlines and trust

A client may time out before the requested wait completes, close the connection and lose unrelated sessions too. Use asynchronous execute and bounded polling; wait expiry does not kill work, while accepted active-request cancellation does. Completed-request cancellation is inert. No keep-alive guarantee or durable reconnect semantics exist. Filesystem/network access is the host's, and user code can exhaust resources or leak capabilities. Do not accept untrusted code or share privileged credentials. No website endpoint executes Lua.
